HEWGE / PERMANENT PROVENANCE & RECOVERY
14 October 2026

Retention policy
Ownership and security history has no automatic expiry. The dedicated hewge-audit table has no TTL. Each event has an immutable ID, per-entity sequence, previous-event hash, actor pseudonym, action, selected non-contact details, and a hash of the exact business mutations. Approved owner changes include previous/new account pseudonyms, certificate/version, declared value and agreed fee policy. Names, contact details, private inspection narrative, credentials and tag keys are kept out of these events.

Each operation first stores an intent in a private, encrypted, versioned S3 archive with a 36,500-day compliance retention and an indefinite legal hold. Business changes, audit head/event and archive outbox commit in one DynamoDB transaction. A protected committed receipt follows. If that last step fails, the API explicitly reports that the change committed but confirmation is pending; the minute worker retries from the durable outbox. An intent alone never proves a change committed. Failed competing requests can leave immutable uncommitted intents; these are deliberate, not additional transfers. No application permission can remove the hold, delete archive objects/versions or shorten retention. Corrections append events. The retained bucket policy also requires create-only conditional writes and blocks deletion/deletion markers on event keys, so a new version cannot silently replace the visible receipt.

“Forever” is an operational preservation policy, not a literal infinite cloud guarantee. Compliance retention is finite and must be extended before expiry; the legal hold has no expiry but a sufficiently privileged account operator can remove it. Continued account payment, organisational control, encryption-key custody and successor stewardship remain necessary. CloudTrail captures management and registry writes with digest validation in the same protected bucket. Maintain independent exports/checkpoints: a compromised administrator who controls every copy is outside the protection of a hash chain alone. Do not claim independently certified bank-level security or an external security audit.

The studio Audit tab checks the DDB chain against protected intent/receipt copies and exports complete NDJSON history with page-by-page verification. Check pending receipt count after administrative work. An integrity mismatch requires investigation, not “repair” by replacing old events. Entity names include piece:<id>, release:<id>, campaign:<uuid>, security:<account-id> and studio:settings. Existing events predating this deployment were not retroactively immutable; maintain that distinction in any provenance statement.

Consistency and availability
All ownership decisions use strongly consistent reads and conditional DynamoDB transactions in eu-north-1. Directory indexes are eventually consistent and used for list discovery only; every ownership/payment/recovery decision re-reads the authoritative record. DynamoDB distributes data across Availability Zones. There is one write authority. A regional outage pauses writes rather than allowing conflicting owners. There is no second-region active failover, in accordance with the region restriction and low-cost target. Do not add an eventually consistent multi-region writer. MRSC global tables do not support the transactions this workflow requires.

Backup and storage
All three DDB tables have point-in-time recovery, on-demand capacity, deletion protection and retained CloudFormation removal policy. AWS Backup takes daily recovery points at 03:00 UTC with 35-day retention, in hewge-protected-backups. Vault Lock has a seven-day cooling-off period, then cannot be removed; retained recovery points cannot have their retention shortened. The audit archive and private media use SSE-S3, TLS, blocked public access and versioning. Staged uploads expire after a day; accepted media and permanent audit records have no expiry lifecycle. NFC key material is separately encrypted with a retained, rotating KMS key. S3 versioning is not an independent backup account; independent custody remains a governance task.

Costs are usage-based and not guaranteed free. The customer-managed NFC KMS key has a monthly charge; PITR, backup storage, S3 versions/requests, CloudTrail data events, logging, hosting, DNS, alarms, email and authentication also incur charges. Avoid a VPC, NAT, dedicated database instances and multi-region write infrastructure at this scale. No hard spending cap or email budget notification is configured.

Recovery drill / incident procedure
1. Confirm AWS_PROFILE=hewge, STS account 082229154968 and eu-north-1. Preserve evidence and identify the last trustworthy ledger checkpoint, CloudTrail digest and payment state. Never restore directly over a live authoritative table.
2. In a real incident, first stop mutations and the worker and disable checkout. Reconcile Stripe sessions/refunds before restarting; a historical restore must not “undo” real payments or reopen sold originals. Record the incident out of band while the normal writer is stopped.
3. Restore PITR or a protected backup into a NEW isolated table. Keep all consumers pointed at the original until checks finish. Validate keys, counts, sampled known records, ownership/provenance continuity and all ledger/archive hashes. Reapply PITR, TTL only to operational tables, indexes, tags, encryption, deletion protection and IAM as required; restored resources do not inherit every setting.
4. Reconstruct pending committed audit receipts from transactional OUTBOX rows. Compare protected intents/receipts and CloudTrail with restored data. A paid webhook newer than the restore point requires reconciliation before any transfer. Never treat a prepared intent as an approved transfer.
5. Prove only one writer can operate before switching resource references. Retain the original for forensics. Resume reads first, then controlled writes and the worker. Inspect audit receipts and pending payments. Delete disposable drill tables after validation; never delete the protected history to make a test pass.
6. At least quarterly and after schema/security changes, repeat a restore drill and export verified provenance. Preserve independent encrypted copies with controlled succession arrangements. Periodically verify backups actually completed, object legal holds, retention dates, CloudTrail delivery, key availability and expired/ambiguous mail/payment queues. Retention renewal and cross-account/offline custody are operator responsibilities, not an already-deployed second facility.

Admin access and lost authenticators
The studio requires verified admin identity plus a server-verified TOTP challenge, not just a Cognito group claim or an “MFA enabled” flag. Sessions are 15 minutes and bound to the exact ID token. Challenge tickets expire in three minutes and are one-use. Keep the authenticator seed/recovery copy in a secure password manager or offline safe; never in source, browser bookmarks, screenshots or chat.

After enrolment, disabling Cognito MFA does not permit password-only studio re-enrolment. An authorised operator must review identity independently, revoke sessions, capture an auditable account-recovery decision and remove the ADMIN_MFA guard only for the reviewed account before re-enrolling. Do not bypass the guard for ordinary password resets. A break-glass AWS identity with hardware MFA and separately controlled recovery is an account governance responsibility; the web app cannot establish that by itself.

Transfer fee and money custody
The recipient pays 50 basis points (0.5%), rounded to a CHF cent, on the declared resale price. Gifts use the last verified sale value. CHF 1,000 means CHF 5. The quote/policy is fixed at request creation and accepted by the intended verified recipient. Hewge inspects physical identity and entitlement first; card checkout collects only the verification/transfer fee. Signed Stripe webhooks, with matched amount/currency/recipient/attempt, record payment. Hewge's final conditional approval alone changes the owner. Fees below Stripe's CHF 0.50 minimum are blocked for reviewed alternative payment; the system never silently raises the agreed fee.

Expired checkout sessions allow a new attempt only after Stripe confirms expiry. Ambiguous requests retry the same idempotency key; old uncertainty beyond its safe window requires manual reconciliation. Paid transfers cannot simply be cancelled. The studio records a full-fee refund request and blocks approval before contacting Stripe; refund.updated/refund.created webhooks reconcile pending outcomes. Only after success can the request be cancelled. Approved transfers, partial refunds, disputes and a failed/ambiguous refund need operator review, not automatic ownership reversal.

Hewge does not take custody of the garment resale price. Use a suitable third-party regulated custody/escrow provider if escrow is needed, with its own eligibility, fees and contract. Stripe Connect delayed payouts are NOT escrow. Provider fees are separate from the 0.5% service fee; this implementation charges exactly the quoted Hewge fee and does not automatically add a card surcharge. Before enabling, settle tax/fee disclosures, provider terms and real sandbox refund/dispute tests. Payment flag: transferPaymentsApproved=true together with the existing checkout launch prerequisites. Keep it false until those are complete.

Implementation verification
Automated tests cover concurrent audit writers, archive failures before/after commit, protected-copy tampering, private media/reveal checks, quoted fees, fee attempt replay and refund gating, RFC 4493 CMAC/SUN vectors, NFC counter races, studio MFA/session expiry and recovery access. Live synthetic checks exercise Cognito MFA, challenge replay, protected receipts, image upload/preview and conditional ownership changes. Physical chip programming/wash tests and real Stripe sandbox transactions remain separate launch gates.

Engagement consent retention
Confirmed opt-outs also write a non-expiring suppression record containing only the email-address hash and timestamp. Audience preview and the delivery worker check it with strong reads. This prevents expiry of a contact record from reviving an older account opt-in. A later explicit verified opt-in may supersede the timestamp. Account preference saves state the current explicit consent choice; account-profile deletion suppresses older release consent. These suppression records are operational preferences, not permanently locked ownership events.

Personal-data limitation — 14 October 2026
Pseudonymous account references, hashes and timestamps can remain personal data. Indefinite object provenance does not authorise indefinite retention of all identity links or security events. The 100-year compliance lock cannot be shortened for existing objects. Before real transactions, document a necessity/legitimate-interest assessment, privacy risk assessment and specialist review of personal data entering irreversible records; redesign/minimise future events if that assessment cannot justify the locks. Do not silently promise erasure that these locks cannot perform. Direct contact/evidence records remain separate and need reviewed deletion/restriction. The deployment gate provenancePrivacyApproved records completion, not automatic compliance. See docs/legal-and-card-launch.txt.
