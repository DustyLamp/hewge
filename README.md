# HEWGE

Live: **https://hewge.design** · Source: **https://github.com/DustyLamp/hewge**

Hewge pursues magnitude through very few exceptional original works and the meaning and recognition they develop. Artist, designer and producer shape each piece; roles can overlap. Each has a journal page and a place in the archive, whether auctioned or gifted through an agreed process. Purchaser and custodian may be different people. Brand colour `#00FEEF`; sparse copy and prominent artwork. Every size is a distinct one-of-one garment, never stock variants of an identical shirt.

The first public design study is **After Hours**, with a proposed opening bid of **CHF 339**. The editorial name/story are draft creative copy. Final artist, material, size and garment photography are still required. There is **no fixed garment release schedule**: the owner chooses each reveal/opening and closing, with one auction open at a time. Free bidding; highest valid bid wins. No lottery, paid entries, follower weighting or buyer's premium.

The [journey playbook](docs/journey-playbook.md) follows all eight participants through the process, including emotion, context-led instructions at every stage, the founder's introductory video and unresolved handovers. The working [launch playbook](docs/launch-playbook.txt) records the first-release vision: very small production, human artists, meaningful ownership, gifting possibilities, and the experience from presentation to wearing. The [creative commissioning guide](docs/creative-commissioning.txt) develops collaborator curation, the brief, interviews, iteration and costs. Examples remain exploratory; no target geography, demographic, collaborators or launch date are confirmed.

The [concept and administrator playbook](docs/administrator-playbook.md) carries the shared decisions: use newer substantive founder direction when older working documents differ, distinguish intended experience from operational capability, and resolve material conflicts explicitly. Social proof means the standing Hewge earns in society and its intended audience, including recognition by influential and consequential people, genuine wear, recommendations and cultural attention. It is broader than paid ownership or auction activity.

Administrators design each release's mystery and reveal sequence individually. An annual purchasable coffee-table book will cover the year's pieces and how they came to be, with deeper material than the website and exceptional physical design. Complimentary custodian copies, potentially signed by one or several artists, remain future possibilities. The book's annual schedule and publication copies are separate from individually timed, one-of-one garments; its purchase/fulfilment flow is not implemented.

The book contains cleared creative stories, not the internal journey maps. Administration, influence/mystery strategy, commercial negotiations, customer transaction journeys and account/payment/transfer/security procedures stay out. Its detailed editorial boundary is in the administrator playbook.

Register and verify the supplied owner email at `/account`, then visit `/dashboard` and enrol an authenticator. Additional administrators must be granted the Cognito `admins` group by an authorised operator; users cannot grant it to themselves. No owner password or account is created in Git. Cognito currently uses its default verification-email service with a small daily quota; configure SES-backed Cognito email before a larger campaign.

## Architecture and running costs

The dashboard is also the CMS. **Releases** controls each piece’s canonical name, story, gallery, prices and dates; **Pages** controls the homepage and editorial pages at `/pages/<slug>`. Drafts, publication records and live text are in DynamoDB; images and revision snapshots are in private encrypted S3. Page publishing does not require a build. Bidding and CMS mutations run in stateless Lambda functions. See `docs/cms-and-auction-architecture.txt` for the publication workflow, bid receipts, ordering/clock limits, DSQL tradeoffs and one-time content migration. Accepted legal versions remain controlled application releases.

Static React/Vite on Amplify; HTTP API Gateway; ARM Node 22 Lambda; DynamoDB on demand; Cognito identity; SES email. A minute-by-minute EventBridge worker settles auctions and drains the email outbox when enabled. No server, VPC, NAT gateway, Shopify subscription or advertising trackers. All three tables have 35-day point-in-time recovery, daily protected backups and deletion protection. Private media is encrypted and versioned; provenance is separately archived under compliance retention and indefinite legal hold. Logs retain seven days; customer emails and tokens are not deliberately logged.

Tables: `hewge-production` stores expiring subscriptions; `hewge-auctions` stores profiles, releases, bids, transfers, provenance and operational records. `hewge-audit` is a separate non-expiring transactional ledger. `services/platform.ts` is the API/worker entry; `services/membership.ts` handles email verification and limited preference sessions; `services/ownership.ts` handles the transfer lifecycle; `shared/auction.ts` owns public redaction and auction calculations. The old direct-purchase reservation service has been removed.

At published rates checked 13 October 2026, [Stripe Switzerland](https://stripe.com/en-ch/pricing) domestic cards cost **2.9% + CHF 0.30** (CHF 10.13 on CHF 339); [TWINT through Stripe](https://stripe.com/en-ch/pricing/local-payment-methods) costs **1.9% + CHF 0.30** (CHF 6.74), subject to activation. International cards, currency conversion and optional services can cost more. No Shopify fee is needed.

[Amplify hosting](https://aws.amazon.com/amplify/pricing/) lists USD 0.15/GB served and USD 0.023/GB-month stored outside applicable free allowances. Local builds avoid Amplify build minutes. At 1,000 visits loading 2 MB each, bandwidth is roughly USD 0.30; 10,000 such visits roughly USD 3. Other images, API/database use, backups, authentication, email, the alarm and Route 53 add charges. Expect low costs at launch, not guaranteed free hosting or a cost cap. Domain renewal is separate. No email budget alert has been configured.

## Payment and delivery integrity

Bids and updates to the auction head are written in one conditional DynamoDB transaction. At closing, the highest accepted bid forms the sale under the terms and the worker immediately attempts that bidder’s authorised card for the server total. Only that bidder can pay the server-calculated CHF amount plus the stated shipping charge. A fallback checkout is allowed only after the automatic intent is definitively cancelled or confirmed paid; an uncertain or processing charge remains locked. A signed, validated Stripe paid event creates anonymous ownership and a certificate ID. Browser redirects do not prove payment. Dispatch requires a signed certificate, ownership card, seal ID, insurance and tracking.

If checkout creation is ambiguous, the offer stays locked. Reconcile its `offerId`, `checkoutAttempt`, `checkoutStartedAt` and Stripe idempotency key before retrying/reassigning. Never blindly clear a lock or mark a paid piece available. A new bidder is selected only after the recovery deadline, a recorded review reference for lawful termination and required notice, and resolution/cancellation of prior Stripe intents/sessions. They receive a fresh optional offer at their own highest bid; it requires acceptance and is never automatically charged. Delayed payment success blocks reassignment until resolved.

The outbox claims each message before sending. Failed or ambiguous sends require inspection rather than automatic duplicate delivery. Release notifications respect consent and size interest; the latest verified membership or collector-account consent wins, including opt-outs. Legacy/unverified guest-list entries never receive release mailings. Non-expiring address-hash suppression records preserve opt-outs after contact expiry, and consent is rechecked immediately before delivery. Release email TTL is 30 days. Pending membership requests and verification links expire after 48 hours; confirmed membership records expire after 180 days and expired entries are excluded from the dashboard and campaigns. Preference access uses a separate one-hour opaque session, not bidding/admin credentials. `/membership` provides fresh access links; `/privacy` requests an unsubscribe-only confirmation link. Entering someone else’s email cannot overwrite their preferences or remove their subscription. Each address is limited to four verification requests/hour and each IP to twelve; only hashes of tokens and rate-limit identifiers are stored as lookup keys. Links use URL fragments, cleared on arrival, and are consumed only by explicit confirmation. Transactional outbox text is removed after delivery; all membership outbox records expire after 48 hours. Ownership/security provenance has no expiry under the owner’s permanent-history policy. Contact data is stored separately. See `docs/permanent-provenance.txt`.

## Ownership and physical authentication

The intended experience separates purchaser, giver and custodian. Current paid checkout registers the paying winner and uses their billing address for delivery. Direct gift delivery/recipient registration, surprise timing and founding-gift valuation require the separate process described in the administrator and journey playbooks; they are not completed checkout features.

A dispatched paid owner's request names the recipient's email. The recipient signs in with that verified email, accepts within seven days and confirms possession with matching garment seal and certificate numbers. No invitation email is sent in this flow; the owner shares the account link. Both participants can cancel before fee payment; a paid fee must first be refunded through review. The recipient pays 0.5% of the resale price (gifts use the last verified sale value), with payment-provider costs separate. The intended recipient accepts the quote and versioned terms before inspection and payment. Service performance waits 14 days unless the recipient separately requests early performance and acknowledges the effect of full completion on any service withdrawal right. Hewge must separately inspect the original/certificate and entitlement evidence, record its private review reference, collect the recipient’s confirmed fee payment, and approve. Fee-only Stripe checkout and refund reconciliation remain gated until Stripe onboarding, final terms and real sandbox testing are complete; no resale funds are held. The owner change, transfer result and provenance event commit together. Approval defaults to the recipient's chosen anonymous setting. Former owners lose editing access. Expired requests can be cancelled and recreated; records have no automatic deletion TTL.

The public register currently shows the opted-in name or `anonymous bidder`, original registration and approved transfer dates. The latter is an existing UI label, not an accurate role for every future custodian; role-appropriate anonymous wording is required as the gift/resale experience develops. It excludes private emails, account identifiers and evidence. A registry entry is a Hewge record, not a government title registry, copyright transfer, escrow service or automatic proof of possession. Legal title disputes require review outside this workflow.

`docs/authentication.txt` describes the recommended secure NFC tag, numbered hologram and artist-signed certificate, with primary sources. **NFC verification is implemented but disabled pending real hardware commissioning**. The key-registration CLI stores per-tag keys encrypted with KMS; the studio commissions tags and handles reviewed replacements. Hardware programming, secure key custody, garment attachment and physical wash/read tests are still required. Printed QR/serial numbers are copyable. Do not sell a garment as NFC-authenticated until that integration is complete.

## Permanent trail, recovery and activation

See [permanent-provenance.txt](docs/permanent-provenance.txt) for the precise audit guarantees, operational limits, backup/restore procedure, MFA recovery, fee/refund workflow and custody recommendation. Audit events have no TTL. Protected S3 versions have renewable 100-year compliance retention plus an indefinite legal hold; “forever” also requires ongoing custody, funding, retention renewal and independent exports. A hash chain or blockchain does not replace physical authentication. There is one Stockholm write authority with AWS Availability Zone resilience; regional outages pause ownership changes. A second regional deployment or automatic regional failover is not configured.

After physical commissioning, `-c nfcCommissioned=true` enables the public NFC verifier. After payment launch prerequisites and real fee/refund sandbox testing, `-c transferPaymentsApproved=true` enables fee checkout alongside `checkoutEnabled=true`. Neither is enabled by default. Also register Stripe `refund.created` and `refund.updated` webhooks. A processor minimum never silently changes the 0.5% fee. Admin refunds block ownership approval before contacting Stripe. Read the runbook before reconciling uncertain sessions or refunds.

Smoke tests use disposable identities and synthetic ownership/fee fixtures, never real charges. Operational QA records are removed; pseudonymous synthetic audit events remain permanently protected by design. Unit tests and live synthetic tests are not substitutes for a Stripe sandbox certification, an independent security audit, physical garment testing or legal approval.

## Auction terms, privacy and withdrawal

No voluntary change-of-mind returns; mandatory withdrawal, defect, misdescription and non-delivery rights remain. Online-only auction status does not itself remove EU/UK distance-sale rights. `/withdraw` accepts notices without sign-in or marketing consent, records the original receipt time and queues a durable email receipt; the studio has a private withdrawal review queue. It does not automatically issue refunds or alter title. Refund/return handling remains a reviewed operator procedure.

Terms and mandate versions/hashes are captured with each bid. Card verification uses Stripe SetupIntent confirmation, release-bound consent and a delivery address, never a browser success flag. `services/auction-cards.ts` locks ambiguous automatic charges to a persistent idempotency key and prevents payment fallback while another charge might settle. Physical-card checks, SCA, declines, webhooks and refunds still need real Stripe sandbox verification before activation. See `docs/legal-and-card-launch.txt`.
